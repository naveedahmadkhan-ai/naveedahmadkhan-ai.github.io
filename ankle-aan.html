Project 01 · Ankle rehabilitation · 2026

Learning assist-as-needed control with a Fourier neural operator for an ankle rehabilitation robot

After a stroke, the ankle needs practice, but a robot that always moves the foot lets the patient switch off. Assist-as-needed control gives only the help that is needed, yet on our pneumatic, cable-driven robot, with a one-second pressure lag and cables that can only pull, adaptive laws need several repetitions to find that help. In this work, a Fourier neural operator trained in a digital twin predicts the assistance for each patient and exercise in about 2 ms. Its output only shifts the robot's equilibrium point, so stability is proved for any output the network can produce. Across 24 virtual stroke patients, starting from its prediction kept 99% of repetitions in the target band, against 81% from full assistance, and the real robot stayed stable at ±18° with five healthy adults.

01 · THE PROBLEM

Too much help, or too little

Recovery depends on the patient's own effort. Classical assist-as-needed controllers start from full assistance and lower it slowly, repetition by repetition, with gains fixed by hand.

TIME

Every new patient starts from zero

Each patient and each exercise needs a different amount of help, so a controller that starts from full assistance spends many repetitions finding it. Those are repetitions in which the patient is carried rather than trained.

SAFETY

A learned controller must not make the robot unsafe

A neural network can predict the right amount of help, but it can also be wrong. On a pneumatic robot strapped to a person, a wrong prediction must cost at most a few repetitions, never stability.

02 · THE APPROACH

Predict, adapt, and assist through the equilibrium point

The operator predicts the starting level and a support profile once per exercise. Two adaptation laws refine them, and the level reaches the robot only by moving the equilibrium point of a fixed impedance law.

PREDICT

An operator, not a fixed-size network

The exercise is a function of time, so the network learns a map between functions rather than between fixed-size vectors. Its Fourier layers act on the lowest frequency modes of the exercise, so the same weights work at any sampling rate, and one prediction takes 1–2 ms.

ADAPT

Between and within repetitions

Between repetitions the band law moves the level towards the error band ,

and within a movement the boost law adds a temporary increment when the error grows.

05 · STABILITY

Why the robot stays stable, whatever the network predicts

The guarantee does not depend on what the network has learned. A single small-gain test covers every possible level signal, because the assistance enters the loop in one simple way and the network's output is always kept inside the tested range.

06 · REAL ROBOT

A first run on the hardware

The controller was also run on the physical robot with five healthy adults (aged 25–38), each with the leg relaxed. It drove an exercise of ±18°, larger than any exercise in its training range, and the loop stayed stable throughout.
