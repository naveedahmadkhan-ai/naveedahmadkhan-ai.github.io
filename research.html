Research

I work on learning-based control for rehabilitation robots: robots that move a person's limb through therapy exercises and have to do it accurately and safely. Each project below opens on its own page, with the problem, what I built, and what we measured on real hardware.

01 · Upper-limb rehabilitation · 2026

RehabTwin: a calibrated digital twin that teaches a DeepONet to control a rehabilitation robot

I built a digital twin of our upper-limb robot, calibrated it on the real hardware, and used it to teach a DeepONet how to correct any exercise before it starts, so the joints stop lagging and sticking behind the plan.

59–76%lower tracking error than the drive alone
132 + 2bench runs and volunteer tests

02 · Ankle rehabilitation · 2026

Energy shaping with an attention-augmented neural operator for an ankle rehabilitation robot

The controller builds an energy landscape around the ankle's target posture, so the robot's torque comes from its slope. An attention-augmented Fourier neural operator re-tunes the landscape for each person, spending less energy and keeping the interaction safe.

20.0%energy-efficiency gain (FNO 16.0%)
10volunteers, six ankle exercises

03 · Ankle rehabilitation · 2025

Reinforcement learning that plans energy-efficient ankle exercises for stroke survivors

A PPO agent, guided by a musculoskeletal model and the energy exchanged between patient and robot, reshapes each exercise path so it stays accurate while lowering joint torque, muscle strain and patient effort.

51.3%joint torque reduction after training
10stroke patients
